
1. LP token pricing must use the actual circulating supply, not the total supply which may include pre-minted or protocol-owned
   tokens.
2. The oracle price must always be a positive value before being used in any collateral or borrowing calculation.
3. In composite oracle paths, intermediate price decimal counts must be tracked and normalized at each multiplication boundary
   to prevent compounding precision errors.
4. Oracle price reads must not occur during a callback context where the underlying price source's state is temporarily
   inconsistent.
5. Fallback/cached prices must have their own staleness validation. A price that is too old to use from the primary feed is also
   too old to use from a cache.
6. A TWAP oracle source pool must have sufficient liquidity that the cost to sustain a manipulated price for the entire window
   exceeds the maximum extractable value.
7. Feed metadata (decimals, heartbeat) must be queried dynamically when the feed uses an upgradeable proxy, not cached at
   deployment time.
8. Feed metadata (decimals, heartbeat) must be queried dynamically when the feed uses an upgradeable proxy, not cached at
   deployment time.
9. The oracle price used in any financial calculation must have been updated within a protocol-defined heartbeat window
   (e.g., 3600 seconds for ETH/USD).
10. Oracle prices must be sanity-checked against a reference band or previous value to detect corrupted updates before they
    affect financial operations.
11. Liquidations revert when the oracle is unavailable. Unhealthy positions accumulate bad debt because they cannot be liquidated
    during the outage. This can lead to protocol insolvency if the outage coincides with a market crash.
12. Share price must not be determined by a single spot price read from a manipulable oracle within a single transaction; NAV
    computations must use TWAP, staleness checks, or a commit-reveal pattern.
13. The heartbeat parameter must be initialized to a non-zero value in the constructor and bounded within a reasonable range
    matching the Chainlink feed's actual update frequency.
14. In multi-step operations with time delays, the oracle price used for P&L reference (entry price) must be re-fetched at the
    consumption point and settlement must be mandatory or expire after a short window, not optional on the trader's initiative.
15. Price normalization must divide by (token decimals * feed decimals), not token decimals alone. The scale of the divisor must
    equal the combined scale of the multiplied operands.
16. A TWAP oracle must use an observation window long enough that the cost of sustaining a manipulated price for the entire
    window exceeds the attacker's potential profit.
